If every U.S. state were a country

Every U.S. state locks up more of its people than almost every other developed country. Only Taiwan is higher than a few states. Massachusetts has the lowest rate of any state: 241 people per 100,000. That is still 1.7 times the United Kingdom's rate, and 7 times Japan's.

How to read it: longer bars mean more people locked up for every 100,000 residents. Grey bars are U.S. states. Orange bars, with bold names, are other developed countries. The purple bar is the whole U.S. How to use it: hover over a bar to see details. Or press Tab to reach the chart, then use the up and down arrow keys. Pick a state from the list to find it.

About the data

U.S. states and the whole U.S.: Prison Policy Initiative, States of Incarceration: The Global Context 2024. It counts everyone locked up: in state prisons, local jails, federal prisons, youth centres, immigration detention and more.

Other countries: the IMF's "advanced economies". Their rates come from the World Prison Brief, through Our World in Data. We used the newest year for each country (2023 to 2026).

Why the U.S. is 614 here but 542 on the map page: the World Prison Brief gives the U.S. 542 (2023). This page uses the Prison Policy Initiative's 614, so the whole U.S. is counted the same way as its states.
