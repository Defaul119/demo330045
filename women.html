Most women in U.S. local jails have not been convicted

On a typical day, about 93,000 women are held in U.S. local jails. That is nearly half of the 190,600 women and girls locked up in the country. Of the women held for local authorities, about 51,400 out of 84,500 (61%) have not been convicted.

How to read it: start in the centre and read outwards. Each ring splits the ring inside it into smaller groups. The wider a piece, the more women it holds.     About the data

    Source: Prison Policy Initiative, Women's Mass Incarceration: The Whole Pie 2024. We copied the numbers from its chart of women in jails. The 190,600 total is from the same report.

    Rounding: each group's number is the sum of the offences inside it, rounded to the nearest 100. The report rounds its numbers, so some of our totals are a little different from the report's (for example, it shows 84,000 held for local authorities; the offences add up to 84,500). Two offences with 0 women (convicted of rape, convicted of immigration offences) are not drawn.

    "Not convicted" means the woman is in jail but has not been found guilty of the charge she is held for. Most are waiting for their trial.
