The U.S. holds 70% of the developed world's prisoners

We looked at 40 rich, developed countries. The United States has 31% of their people. But it has 1.86 million of their 2.65 million prisoners. That is more than all the other 39 countries put together.

How to read it: each purple circle is a country. The bigger the circle's area, the more people it has in prison. How to use it: hover over a circle, or press Tab to move to it, to see exact numbers. Use the arrow keys to go to the next circle. Scroll or use the + and − buttons to zoom, and drag to move the map.

About the data

Countries: the 40 countries the IMF calls "advanced economies". We left out Puerto Rico because it is part of the U.S.

Prison rates: World Prison Brief, through Our World in Data. We used the newest year for each country (2023 to 2026).

Number of people in prison: our estimate. It is the rate × the population ÷ 100,000. Population is from Our World in Data (2023 if the rate is newer).

Map: Natural Earth, through world-atlas. Some places are too small to show as land (Hong Kong, Macao, Singapore, Malta, Andorra and San Marino), but they still have a circle. The smallest circles are drawn a little bigger than their true size so you can hover over them.
